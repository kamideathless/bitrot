01 — Why it is different

The arena is an automaton

Every generation, a clean cell may rot — and the chance climbs steeply with how many rotten neighbours it has. Rot never dies on its own. Purging clears a disc and scars the ground so it cannot be reborn for a while. The board is something you garden, not something you dodge.

Burning rebuilds pixels

Every Friend you rescue arrives corrupted — the pixels missing from the portrait are the pixels the rot ate. Burning RF restores them ten percent at a time, in an order fixed per Friend, and those tokens are destroyed. You can see exactly what the burn bought.

02 — One dive

Hold the line

Move, purge, dash. Shards are currency and energy; the rot eats them if you are slow. Sectors tighten every 45 seconds and every purge starts costing more.

Get them out

Capsules surface every 19 seconds and survive six seconds buried — purge one free. Rescuing is also the only thing that restores your integrity.

Cash the run

Every RF you earned, itemised. Then the only question that matters: spend it on gear, or burn it on a Friend?

05 — Under the hood

  • Everything renders into a single Uint8Array of 0s and 1s, blitted with integer scaling. Shading exists only as ordered dithering, so rot density reads as age.
  • The purge shockwave is literally an XOR of the framebuffer. The inverted palette is exact, not themed.
  • Both fonts — 5×7 and a 4×6 micro — are hand-drawn bitmaps. No webfont, no antialiasing. The headings on this page use them too.
  • The picture is presented through Scale2x, which rounds off single-pixel corners on the way to the screen. Still pixel art, just a finer grain — and a toggle for anyone who wants hard edges.
  • All audio is generated live by a small square-wave synth. No audio files.
  • No build step. Plain ES modules, served as-is. The repository root is the site.
  • The logical screen presents at 960×640 — the same viewport FriendSDK reference games use.

One dive is about ninety seconds. You will lose the first few. That is the point.

All balances, purchases and rewards in BITROT are simulated. No wallet is connected, no chain is touched, and no real $RAREFRIENDS is minted, spent or burned. Balances are rows in the game's own database — real records, simply not tokens. Friend portraits are procedural placeholders, not Rare Friends artwork. MIT licensed.
